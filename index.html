Ragi sangati& Natu kodi

Ragi Sangati is a traditional Andhra and Rayalaseema staple made from a wholesome mix of rice and finger millet flour, usually paired with spicy country chicken (Natu Kodi) or rich gravies

Rayalaseema Biryani – Dry, Fiery Style

Drier, darker, and hotter than traditional biryani—this is Kadapa's signature spice-belt recipe. Made in oil with fiery chilies and zero fluff, it is pure, high-heat comfort food.

Natu Kodi

Authentic, free-range desi chicken raised in local villages. Leaner and firmer than regular chicken, it is slow-cooked into a rich, fiery tamarind gravy packed with true Andhra flavor.

Andhra Kodi Pulao

Andhra kodi pulao is a one-pot chicken rice in which basmati and chicken cook together, so the rice takes its flavour straight from the meat’s juices as it steams. It is lighter and less layered than biryani, built on whole spices, shallots and fresh green-chilli heat rather than a separate dum stage.

"Ulavacharu Biryani

Most biryanis begin with rice and meat. This one begins with a soup. Ulavacharu biryani takes ulavacharu (a slow-simmered horse-gram extract that Coastal Andhra households eat with rice and ghee) and layers it through the biryani instead, so the rice cooks in that dark, tangy broth. The result is a biryani that tastes of the earth rather than only of chilli heat, earthy and sour and deep in a way a standard Andhra biryani is not.

Chepala Pulusu, Royyala Iguru & Coastal Fry and Rice

Along the Krishna and Godavari coast, a seafood meal is rarely one dish on its own. A bowl of chepala pulusu (fish in a tamarind-soured gravy) sits and darkens as it waits. Beside it, royyala iguru (thick, slow-cooked prawns) has reduced until the oil beads on the surface. Somewhere near the edge, the fish fry and prawn fry rarely reach the table whole, because someone always gets to them first. Coastal Andhra reads these four as one spread, not four recipes to look up separately.
